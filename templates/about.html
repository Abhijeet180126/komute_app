Cars were meant for people, not empty seats.

Most cars on the road are travelling with three or four empty seats. Meanwhile, somewhere along that same route, a student is paying triple for the bus, a parent is squeezing onto an overcrowded train, and a commuter is wishing the trip felt less lonely.

Komüte makes it easy for drivers to fill those empty seats with people heading the same way — splitting costs, cutting emissions, and turning solo commutes into something a bit more human.

Why we exist

The car next to you is probably going your way.

We started Komüte after one of our co-founders calculated that on her daily commute to downtown, she passed an average of 1,400 cars travelling the same direction — almost all of them with empty seats.

Buses skipped her route. The train was packed and expensive. And yet here were 1,400 strangers, every single morning, with seats to spare. We thought: what if we just introduced everyone?

How we're different

Built for sharing, not for surge pricing.

Komüte isn't a taxi service in disguise. We don't dispatch drivers. We don't take a percentage of inflated peak-hour fares. Drivers are everyday commuters and travellers offering seats on trips they were already taking.

That's why prices stay reasonable. That's why the chat is friendlier. And that's why every ride is a real conversation between two people heading the same way — not a transaction between an algorithm and a stranger.

Where we're headed

From one Canadian commute, to a continent.

We launched in Canada, but our roads don't stop at the border. Over the next two years Komüte is rolling out across the US — starting with the northern commuter corridors and expanding city by city.

Down the line: smarter route matching, dynamic eco-rewards, and partnerships with employers who want to help their teams commute more sustainably. The destination keeps moving. The mission stays the same.
